# Palette library (reference, not mandatory)

About 60 curated palettes, grouped by mood, plus contrast-checked shortlists for 15 kinds of project (below). **Use only when there's no brand colour.** Start from the shortlist for the kind of project (below), or from the mood groups when the ask gives a feel ("warm and moody", "fresh", "bold"). Then **adapt**: shift tones to the business, assign roles (base, ink, accent, surface), fix contrast for text, and usually use 2–3 of the colours, not all. An existing brand always wins. Never mention these names on the site.

Colours were sampled from the palette images (they match the printed hex codes closely). Palettes that came with a soft gradient version are marked ◐: they also work as code-built gradient/mesh backgrounds (CSS radial gradients, or a canvas mesh) for heroes and footer scenes.

## Shortlists by kind of project

Use these when **no brand colours were given**. An existing brand, or a file being rebuilt with colours of its own, always wins, and then there is nothing to shortlist or swap.

**How to choose (never one option):**
1. Find the field below (or the nearest one) and read its lean, usual colours, cliché and what stands out.
2. Shortlist **three** sets. Include at least **one that fits** the field and **one that stands out**. The sets come from this field, a neighbouring field or the mood groups above.
3. Pick by the brief's wording, then by the field's lean:
   - "trustworthy", "classic" or "professional" leans fit;
   - "different", "bold", "fun" or "unlike other X" leans stand out;
   - with no signal, follow the field's lean.
4. Pick the best match for this subject, not the first listed and not the one you always reach for.
5. Name the other two in your finishing note as one-line swaps.
6. Mix by role if it helps: the main colour from one set and the pop from another. Keep 2–3 main colours.

**Roles:**
- **Base** is the page background.
- **Ink** is text.
- **Main** is the signature colour, used for colour blocks, hero areas and key actions.
- **Pop** is a small highlight used sparingly: tags, key numbers, one detail.
- Sets marked *(dark)* use a dark base. There, *main* is the panel surface and *pop* carries the colour, unless the set says otherwise (ev-b Single Red uses red as the main colour).
- Surfaces and tints can be mixed from base and main.

**Contrast is pre-checked** for every set:
- ink on base is 7:1 or more;
- the "Text on main" column gives the colour to use for text on main blocks (4.5:1 or more);
- text on pop reaches 4.5:1 with base or ink.

A few colours were nudged slightly lighter or darker to pass, so treat the hex values as final. Recheck any tint you mix yourself.

**Honesty:** field notes describe common patterns, not laws. They're drawn from a colour-by-field synopsis of published industry analyses and design-system guidance. Brand names there are cues only; never copy a brand's palette.

### Status colours (dashboards, ops, any tool with states)
Use these in tools, and **always pair them with an icon or label**. Never use colour alone. Never use a status colour as the accent or brand colour. All are 4.5:1 or more on white and on light grey `#F5F6F8`. For fills, use a light tint of the colour behind dark text.

## Using a palette well
- Base + ink + one accent is usually enough; the other swatches become surfaces or states.
- Check text contrast (4.5:1 for body text); darken or lighten a swatch rather than dropping it.
- Neon swatches (lime, magenta, cyan) work as accents or on dark bases, rarely as large light backgrounds.
- Don't reuse the same palette on consecutive builds; the goal is range, not a house palette. A new chat can't remember the last build, so range comes from shortlisting and choosing, not memory.
