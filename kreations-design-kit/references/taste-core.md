# Taste core

The principles in SKILL.md §6 always apply. This file adds the evidence, the starting options, the home-base references, color and writing voice.

"Seen" means clearly visible in the reference clips. "Suggested" means an extrapolation or one of the kit's preferences.

## Principles, with evidence

1. **One strong focal idea leads.** In the clips it's a sculpture (v02), a jacket (v03), a character (v18), a house (v07, v11), a person (v10, v12), or the type itself (v08's "TIMELESS FURNITURE/"). In a tool, the focal idea is the result.
2. **Big against small.** Almost every clip pairs a huge subject or headline with tiny labels. Take the *ratio*, not the tiny sizes. The kit's floors are labels of at least 12px and touch targets of at least 44px, plus real contrast.
3. **One visual world.** v03 is technical all the way down (grid, labels, exploded diagram). v06 carries coffee packaging, a receipt and a clock into the interface. v16 swaps the whole color world with each product.
4. **Depth and staging.** The subject crosses the headline in 7 of 22 (v02, v10, v12, v16, v18, v19, v20). Others use light, overlap and translucent panels (v07, v11, v15, v21, v22). The technique is always optional.
5. **Space does work.** v04, v07, v11 and v13 let big calm areas hold one line or image.
6. **Websites move in chapters.** Dramatic scene → calm reading surface: v01 (particles → white FAQ), v05, v09, v18 (dark → cream services), v04.
7. **Motion explains or connects**, at the level set in SKILL.md §3. A complete static version always exists.
8. **A real ending for journeys.** Seen: v04's navy footer, v08's red footer, v10's blue booking panel, v12's giant email, v18's lilac invitation.

## Brand and richness (from test round 2)

- Brand and theming outrank everything here: follow the existing brand; if there isn't one, ask (SKILL.md §0.5).
- Richness follows the brand and the ask and stays even across the page. "Showy" has a soft cap: one big opening scene, calmer sections after, 2–3 main colours, one main 3D subject. Playable shapes are a plus.
- Shorter copy, same rich design. One-pagers stay one cohesive, premium piece.

## Round 3 additions (from testing and review)

- The opening and the body get equal polish (a portfolio test: the new opening was loved, but the earlier body was preferred).
- The name or title is never hidden by objects in the opening.
- Crisp fonts for professional work: the one-pager lost points for a soft, informal serif. Font library: `fonts.md`.
- Small textures (dot grid, fine grain, hairline grid) are welcome when they fit.
- Palettes for reference only: `palettes.md`. Footer scenes: `moves.md` (n12).

## Home-base references (current go-tos)

Lean on these when a request doesn't point at a specific tone. Take the **cues**, not the colors, imagery or copy.

**forme (v18), Playful portfolio**

- Two rows of work drift in opposite directions
- Rounded panels stack over each other as you scroll, changing surface color (dark → cream → lilac)
- Numbered service rows (01–05)
- "Start a project" opens a brief builder with copy/download controls (not a mailto). The controls are visible in the clip; a completed export wasn't seen.

**Northwall (v13), Calm & premium / Practical company site**

**Vesper Studio (v21), Calm & premium / Gallery studio page**

- The optional *inset frame* move (`moves.md`) was inspired by how this clip was presented. It is not a default.

## Color

- Give every color a role: base, ink, one accent, and maybe a secondary surface.
- Build the palette from the new subject (its materials, place, audience), then apply the tone's approach.
- Product-driven pages may swap the whole palette per selection (v16, v19) or use a small family (v01's mint + violet).
- Plan light and dark surfaces deliberately. Many clips alternate them to mark chapters (v05, v13, v18).
- Avoid unplanned rainbows, and avoid defaulting to purple-on-white or cream-and-terracotta just because they're safe.

## Type

- Pair a characterful display face with a quiet text face; add a mono or small-caps face for labels if the tone wants one.
- **Choose type from the subject, audience and existing identity first**, then the tone. No face or treatment is a house default. Before settling, ask: "Does this fit this project, or am I repeating the last output?"
- Match the *character* of a reference, not the exact font. Heavy condensed faces for Loud poster, a refined serif or thin grotesk for Gallery, a clean grotesk for Practical, mono for Technical, something friendly or rounded for Playful.
- Mixing is welcome when it's deliberate: v08 pairs a condensed display with an editorial serif; v06 pairs heavy caps with a handwritten word.

## Writing voice (part of the tone)

| Voice | Seen in | Feels like |
|---|---|---|
| Plain-spoken, practical | v04 | "Two of us come out, photograph everything we find…" |
| Dry, understated | v08 | Short declaratives, product facts, gentle wit |
| Forceful first person | v12 | Short, confident, principle-driven |
| Patient, literary | v21 | Calm, confident, few words |
| Cheeky | v10 | A hook with a twist ("…look like a friend filmed them. Nobody did.") |

Match the voice to the tone and subject. Whatever the voice, **keep copy short and scannable**: headlines of a few words, one or two short sentences under each, no paragraph walls. Write real, specific copy for the new project, never invent business facts or promises (prices, turnaround, revisions, guarantees, results), and never reuse the clips' lines.
