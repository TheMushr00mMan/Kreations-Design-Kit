# Website categories

Pick the category by the page's **job**: what the visitor should do. Section lists are **menus, not fixed orders**. Pick what the content needs and skip the rest.

Then **fit the kind of business**: before styling, name in one line what this type of business usually looks and feels like to its customers and keep one or two of those cues. A tattoo studio should read as a tattoo studio even when the request says "clean and reserved".

Markers: **H** = only the hero was seen (use it for hero ideas, not full-page structure). **X** = a hybrid that sits between categories. **Bridge** = an example that deliberately blends two categories.

---

## Portfolio: judge a body of work, then reach out

Can belong to a person, a studio or a company.

**Menu:** identity hero (name or statement + subject) → the work (wall, drifting rows, stacked cards, featured project) → point of view (beliefs, principles) → capabilities (numbered services) → proof (stats, quotes, logos) → closing invitation (big email, brief builder).

**Use when:** "show my work", "portfolio", "present our studio", "about me", "personal site".

**Variants**
- *Sign-up-first / coming soon* (v21): email capture under the headline, then beliefs and what we make.
- *Bridge to Company* (v07): a work-led flow that ends in a quote. In the clip the quote action is in the top nav and the page ends on a giant studio wordmark; a closing quote form is our adaptation.

**Examples:** v12 Loud poster · v18 Playful (home base) · v21 Calm/Gallery (home base) · v07 Calm/Gallery (bridge) · v01 Cinematic (X: studio / product / showcase) · n02 Swiss · n08 Loud poster (studio)

---

## Company site: understand an offer, then take a practical next step

Book, enquire, order or visit.

**Menu:** offer hero + action → about + stat row → services / menu / listing facts → process steps → recent work or testimonials → practical info (location, hours, specs, delivery times) → reassurance (licences, guarantees, response time: only real ones supplied by the user, or marked placeholders) → enquiry, booking or order.

**Use when:** "site for my business", "book a…", a café, trade, firm or local shop, "sell this house", "sell my service".

**Variants**
- *Local business* (v04 roofing, v06 café): live context strip, menu with prices, hours and locations.
- *Firm* (v13, home base): projects, process, team, detailed enquiry form.
- *Single listing* (v11): one house, with emotion → facts → fit → book a viewing. A product image doesn't mean it needs a cart.
- *Packaged service* (v10, bridge to Portfolio): a sales page that flows like a portfolio (method, deliverables, process, rates, "book a batch").

**Examples:** v04, v11, v13 Calm/Practical · v06, v10 Playful · n05 Calm (image-led listing) · n07 Playful/Scrapbook (residency)

## Portfolio vs Company

The request decides which structure leads. "Show my work" → Portfolio. "Sell / book my service" → Company. Either can borrow the other's sections. When unsure, ask yourself whether the page mainly helps the visitor **judge a body of work** or **understand an offer and take a practical next step**. v07 and v10 are the blending references.

## Product page: understand software or tech, then try it

**Menu:** promise + demonstrator → proof (logos, ratings, stats) → how it works / features → use cases → research or credibility (v15's "What we publish") → pricing or access → FAQ → start / demo / request access.

**Use when:** a landing page for an app, tool, AI, API or platform. This is the *marketing* page. For the tool itself, see `tools-documents.md`.

**Product-native (preferred when there's a real product):** let the product's own interface language (its controls, timeline, cards, readouts, icons) become the page's design language: section frames, inline glyphs in headlines, real UI as proof. The favourite example is n09 (Butter); n03 and n01 do it more quietly. Use the user's real UI or clearly marked mock UI, never invented metrics.

**Examples:** v05, v09 Cinematic · v15 Technical · v01 Cinematic (X) · n09 Playful+Technical (product-native) · n03 Technical · n01 Calm/Practical · n10 Loud poster+Playful (community)

## Case studies and process stories

When a page tells how a consultant or studio works, show the **method in general terms** (e.g. Listen → Align → Activate as their way of working) and use one real case study as **proof** with its results, rather than making the whole section about one client's specifics.

## Store: choose a product and buy it

**Menu:** product or collection hero → categories / options → grid with prices, filters and delivery labels → details or construction → in-context view (room, outfit, setting) → buying reassurance (sizing, care, shipping, returns) → newsletter. On full storefronts the bag stays in the nav.

**Use when:** "shop", "drop", "collection", "pick a color / model", or a product launch where **buying** is the goal.

**Small makers' shops:** when there are more products than fit on one screen, a product shelf that scrolls sideways (with room for more products than fit on screen) reads as a real shop and lets the range grow; pair it with clear colour-blocked sections for story, visit and shipping.

**Examples:** v03 Technical · v08 Loud poster (with an editorial serif voice) · v19 H Loud poster · v14 H Calm/Gallery · v16 H Playful · n06 Playful (subscription)

---

## Showcase: admire or explore one subject

**Menu:** the whole subject → chapters (a new angle, material or feature each) → numbered close-ups with captions → context or environments → a chapter tracker or menu → a minimal ending (or credits).

**Use when:** **exploring or admiring is the main job**: concept pieces, lookbooks, design studies, an "Apple product page" reveal with no sale. Words like "reveal", "feature tour", "case study" or "like Apple" are *presentation cues*. If the visitor should buy, use Store; if they should try software, use Product; if they should judge someone's work, use Portfolio. Any of those can borrow Showcase chapters.

**Examples:** v02 Cinematic · v22 Loud poster (with Technical details) · v01 Cinematic (X) · n11 Loud poster/Raw (film) · n04 Cinematic (experimental teaser)

## Gaps

The category × tone combinations without an example (e.g. Product in Playful, Showcase in Calm) are open. Build them by applying the tone to the category's menu, and say it's an extrapolation. Don't invent a reference that doesn't exist.
