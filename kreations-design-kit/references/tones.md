# Tones

A tone is the page's **feel**. It never changes the job. Pick one **primary** tone. Borrowing one secondary quality is optional (e.g. v08 is Loud poster with an editorial serif voice); zero is normal. Every tone's motion ideas sit **within the motion level chosen from the request** (SKILL.md §4), never above it. The colors listed are what the clips happened to use. Build the actual palette from the new subject.

---

## Calm & premium

Two flavors under one name.

### Practical
- **Use when:** facts, trust and an easy next step matter more than spectacle: local businesses, firms, listings, service pages, most tools.
- **Look:** light surfaces; real-world photography (or marked slots); one dark action color (the clips use navy and black); rounded image corners; compact floating nav; tidy rows and rules; stat rows that count up; a clear, low-effort form.
- **Motion:** subtle to one moment. Words darkening as you scroll, count-ups, accordion reveals, a pinned process with the active step sharp.
- **Voice:** plain-spoken, specific, reassuring.
- **Clips:** v04, v11, v13 (home base); n01, n05.

### Gallery
- **Use when:** the work, space or voice should reward attention: studios, portfolios, premium products, teasers.
- **Look:** a refined serif, a thin grotesk or a quiet sans (pick from the subject); patient pacing; muted palettes (the clips use sage, warm wood and deep navy); words placed in corners around a central subject; frosted captions; generous empty space. Dark or light.
- **Motion:** one moment. A slow camera move toward the subject, a room assembling, a nav that hides on scroll.
- **Voice:** patient and confident, still short enough to skim.
- **Optional treatment:** a serif headline with one softer italic word (v21). Use it only when it clearly suits the project; never reach for it by default.
- **Clips:** v07, v14, v21 (home base).

## Cinematic
- **Use when:** you want wonder, or need to make something unfamiliar feel real (tech products, showcases, reveals).
- **Look:** light and material doing the work: particles, glow, light ribbons, chrome, iridescence; big thin type. In the clips 3 of 4 open dark (v05 opens on saturated violet), and they often switch to light reading surfaces (v01, v05, v09). **Dark is not required.**
- **Motion:** one moment to full journey. A persistent subject changing form, cards tilting into 3D rings, heroes shrinking into cards.
- **Voice:** short promises, big claims backed by a calm explanation.
- **Clips:** v01, v02, v05, v09; n04 (experimental, only when asked).

## Loud poster
- **Use when:** a strong point of view or identity: personal brands, drops, collections, bold showcases.
- **Look:** heavy display type (condensed in v08, v12, v19, v22; wide in v20); one hot color field or accent chosen to fit the subject; the subject crossing the letters; marquees; hazard stripes or index numbers as motifs.
- **Motion:** one moment to full journey. A letter-by-letter entrance, a scene swapping behind fixed type, a % loader.
- **Voice:** forceful, short, first person or imperative.
- **Clips:** v08, v12, v19, v22 (v20 as a pattern); n08, n10.

## Loud poster flavour: Raw / documentary
- **Use when:** the user asks for "edgy", "dark", "gritty", "raw", "underground" (music, film, nightlife, streetwear, activism; a tattoo studio only when that vibe is asked for). Never a default for any business type.
- **Look:** a two-colour world (off-white + near-black, maybe one muted accent); black-and-white or heavily graded photography with grain; tall condensed caps; poster conventions (credit blocks, laurels, index numbers); a photo set inside a letter.
- **Motion:** subtle to one moment: hard cuts, a slow grain drift, a title that assembles. Nothing bouncy.
- **Voice:** blunt, short, documentary.
- **Clips:** n11 (Union).

---

## Swiss / typographic
- **Use when:** the type or grid should lead: "typographic", "Swiss", "editorial", "type-led", "let the type do the work", or designer/developer portfolios that want type as the identity. A plain "minimal" or "clean" business page does **not** trigger it (that stays in its own tone, just restrained).
- **Look:** monochrome (black on white, or reversed) with at most one accent; huge tight grotesk; a visible structure (one hairline rule, a strict column grid, corner labels); tables and lists as design elements; images small or contained.
- **Motion:** subtle to one moment: words rising into place, a % loader, one playful element inside the strict layout (e.g. a 3D sphere of work thumbnails).
- **Voice:** short, confident statements.
- **Clips:** n02 (bleibtgleich); n08 shares the caps-and-grid discipline.
- Differs from Loud poster: restraint and structure instead of hot colour fields.

---

## Technical
- **Use when:** inputs, specs or state should feel inspectable: technical products, gear, dashboards, calculators, developer tools.
- **Look:** mono labels; grids or dot grids; index numbers; status readouts; bracket corners; bordered cells; exploded or labelled diagrams.
- **Motion:** subtle to one moment. Text that scrambles or types in, readouts updating, a block cursor.
- **Voice:** precise, spec-like, terse.
- **Clips:** v03, v15 (v17 for pixel-type details); n03 (with a Cinematic glow).
- Readouts must mean what they say. Never fake live data.

---

## Playful
- **Use when:** exploring is part of the appeal: personal portfolios, cafés and consumer brands, product pickers, fun tools.
- **Look:** characters and toy-like objects; color worlds that swap; handwriting next to heavy type; brand props doubling as UI (a receipt, a clock); cursor tricks; rounded stacking panels.
- **Motion:** one moment to full journey. A head turning, a brush revealing a second layer, letters flipping as a color changes.
- **Voice:** warm, cheeky, a little self-aware.
- **Clips:** v06, v10, v16, v18 (home base); n06, n09, n10.

### Playful flavour: Scrapbook / collage
- **Use when:** "artsy", "fun", "hand-made": art studios, residencies, creative communities, zines.
- **Look:** cut-out stickers and taped notes (code-built or the user's own assets), irregular polygon image masks, flat colour cards that change on hover, small live details (local time, weather ticker), bracketed mono labels.
- **Motion:** subtle to one moment: stickers settling, hover colour swaps.
- **Clips:** n07 (Kosa).

## Picking when the request is vague
- Mood words decide first ("calm", "fun", "techy", "bold", "cinematic"). "Clean" or "reserved" limits the *amount* of decoration and motion, not the character. "Edgy", "dark", "gritty" point to the Raw flavour; "typographic", "Swiss", "type-led" point to Swiss (plain "minimal" does not).
- Fit the kind of business: name what it usually looks and feels like (a tattoo studio: ink, skin, flash sheets, bold linework; a bakery: warmth, flour, handwriting) and keep one or two of those cues inside the chosen tone.
- Then the subject and audience: a trade business leans Practical; a studio leans Gallery; a gadget leans Cinematic or Technical; a personal brand leans Loud poster or Playful.
- Otherwise start from a home-base reference: forme (Playful), Northwall (Practical) or Vesper Studio (Gallery). Say which one you chose.
